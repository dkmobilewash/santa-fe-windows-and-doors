Pueblo Revival & Territorial

Deep openings need a custom fit

Pueblo Revival homes, with their rounded parapets, vigas and earth-toned walls, and Territorial homes, with brick-capped parapets and painted wood trim, are often built with thick adobe or frame-and-stucco walls. Window openings can be deep, slightly out of square, and different from one another.

Replacement windows have to be fitted to those openings. We measure each one individually and plan:

  • Window size and placement within the wall depth
  • Jamb extensions and deep sills (wood, tile or plaster)
  • How interior plaster and exterior stucco returns will be finished
  • Profiles and divided-lite patterns that suit the style of the house

Historic districts

Working with the H-Board

Santa Fe has several historic districts, including the Downtown and Eastside district. In these districts, exterior changes such as replacing windows or doors may need review and approval from the city before work begins.

Depending on the project and how your building is classified, review may be handled by the city's Historic Preservation Division staff or go before the Historic Districts Review Board (H-Board). Requirements can cover materials, divided-lite patterns, trim and how much of the original fabric is kept.

We can help you through the process:

  • Check early whether your address is in a historic district
  • Recommend products and details likely to fit the guidelines
  • Provide product specifications, drawings and photos for your application
  • Plan the schedule so nothing is ordered before it's approved
Please note: approval decisions belong to the City of Santa Fe. We can't promise an approval or a timeline, but we'll help you put together a clear, complete application and adjust the plan if the city asks for changes.
