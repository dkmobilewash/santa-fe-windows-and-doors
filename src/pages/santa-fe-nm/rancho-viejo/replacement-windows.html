Rancho Viejo replacement windows

New windows for Rancho Viejo homes, without cutting into the stucco

Like most newer stucco homes, Rancho Viejo houses typically have vinyl windows that were nailed to the framing before the stucco went on. After years of high-desert sun, many now have cloudy glass and stiff sashes. The good news: they can usually be replaced from the outside edge of the old frame, leaving your stucco and interior drywall alone.

Signs your original windows are done

Milky glass

A haze between the panes that never wipes off means the seal has failed and the insulating gas is gone.

Hard to slide

Sashes that stick or jump their track after years of grit and UV-stiffened weatherstrip.

Dust on the sill

A fine line of dust along the inside sill after a windy day shows where air is getting through.

Chalky frames

Vinyl that has faded or turned chalky on the sunny sides of the house.

How we swap windows in a stucco house

For most openings we cut the old window's nailing fin free, lift the unit out, and set a replacement with a flat exterior flange that covers the gap against the existing stucco. It's sealed and trimmed so the opening looks finished from the street, and there's no patching or repainting of the wall.

If an old frame has let water into the wall, we find out before ordering, and plan a full-frame replacement with flashing for that opening instead.

Getting more than the builder installed

Original windows in a production home were picked on price. When you replace them, it's worth looking at heavier frames that resist warping in full sun, glass coatings tuned to which way each wall faces, and screens and hardware that hold up to the wind coming across the open land. Rancho Viejo window questions

Will replacing the windows damage my stucco?

Not with the method we use for most Rancho Viejo homes. The new window covers the old opening from outside and is sealed against the existing stucco, so the wall itself isn't cut. Full-frame work is only planned where hidden damage calls for it.

Do I have to replace every window at once?

No. Many owners start with the sunny west and south sides where the windows have aged fastest, then do the rest later. We'll keep notes on your sizes and colors so a second phase matches the first.

Does the HOA have to approve replacement windows?

It depends on what's changing and on your association's current rules, so check with them first. If approval is needed, we supply the specifications and colors you'll need to submit.
