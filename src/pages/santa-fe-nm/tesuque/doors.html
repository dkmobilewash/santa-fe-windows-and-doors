Wildfire-aware door details

Gaps around and under exterior doors can let wind-blown embers inside. Tight weatherstripping, a well-fitted sweep and a solid threshold close those paths, and they also happen to keep out drafts and dust.

For glazed doors, tempered glass is standard, and dual-pane units add another layer of protection from heat. We'll walk through options that make sense for your lot.

Big doors take planning. Multi-panel doors and oversize units can have longer lead times and may need extra hands on install day, especially on a steep or long driveway. We'll be upfront about both.

Tesuque door questions

Will a big sliding door hold up to the wind on our ridge?

A quality door rated for the exposure, installed with a properly sloped and sealed sill, handles strong wind well. We look at your elevation and exposure before recommending a specific door.

Can you install a new door in our old village adobe without damaging the plaster?

We take out the old frame carefully and plan for some patching around the opening. We'll tell you before the work starts how much plaster repair to expect.

Do new doors help with ember protection?

They can help. Tight seals and sweeps reduce gaps, and tempered, dual-pane glass resists heat better than older glazing. No door makes a home fireproof, but these details are worth including.
