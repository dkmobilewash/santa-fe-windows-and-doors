Valley home or hillside home?

Down by the river

Village adobes tend to have smaller, deep-set openings, plenty of summer shade and cool nights as air settles into the valley. Casements and traditional double-hungs in wood or clad frames suit them, with glass chosen for insulating value more than heat control.

Up in the hills

Hillside homes often have big view walls, little shade and full exposure to wind. Large picture units, fiberglass frames that stay stable through hot afternoons, and solar-control glass on the west side usually make the most sense.

Glass choices for piñon-juniper country

Much of the land around Tesuque is covered in piñon and juniper, and wildfire is part of life here. Dual-pane windows with tempered glass hold up to heat far better than single-pane or untempered units, which is why they're commonly recommended for homes near wildland.

No window makes a house fireproof, but we're happy to price tempered glass throughout, and we'll point out any other window-related details worth thinking about.
