Efficient windows in Tesuque

Cold valley nights, sunny ridges: efficient glass for both sides of Tesuque

Cool air drains downhill after sunset, so homes near the river often feel noticeably colder on winter nights than houses higher up. Meanwhile, hillside homes bake in afternoon sun with nothing to shade them. Good glass for Tesuque starts with knowing which of those problems you have.

In the valley: hold the heat in

For a shaded adobe along the river, winter heat loss is usually the bigger issue. Glass with a low U-factor, a warm-edge spacer and careful sealing around the frame do most of the work. Solar control matters less where cottonwoods already shade the house in summer.

On the ridge: keep the afternoon out

A west-facing view wall on an open hillside can overheat a room for hours. There, a low-E coating with stronger solar control pays off, while south glass can stay more open to winter sun. Wind exposure also makes a tight installation essential.

Tesuque efficiency questions

Why does our house by the river feel colder than our friends' place up the hill?

On clear nights, cold air settles into low spots along the valley, so riverside homes can run several degrees cooler. Windows with better insulating glass and tight seals help keep that cold on the outside.

Our view wall makes the living room unbearable on summer afternoons. Will new glass fix it?

Glass with stronger solar control can cut a lot of that heat while keeping the view clear. Pairing it with an overhang or exterior shade, where possible, helps even more.

Does altitude affect windows in Tesuque the same way as in town?

Yes. Insulated glass is ordered with the elevation in mind for any home in the area, and we'll explain how that affects gas-fill options for your units.
