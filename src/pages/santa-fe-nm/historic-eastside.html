Homes in Historic Eastside

Some of Santa Fe's oldest houses

The Eastside, including Canyon Road, Acequia Madre and the streets around them, is one of the oldest parts of Santa Fe. Much of it lies within the city's Downtown and Eastside historic district.

Many homes here are adobe, some added onto over generations, with thick walls, small deep-set windows and openings that vary from room to room. Original wood windows and doors are common, and so are later replacements that no longer fit the character of the house.

Common projects

  • Matching new windows to the style of an older adobe
  • Replacing drafty or rotted wood windows and doors
  • Fitting units to deep, out-of-square openings
  • Repairing original windows: glass, sashes, weatherstripping
  • New entry and courtyard doors in thick walls
Historic-district review. Exterior changes here may need city review, and some go before the Historic Districts Review Board (H-Board). Guidelines can favor repairing original windows or matching their materials and lite patterns. We help you prepare; the decision is the city's.

Historic Eastside questions

Can I replace the windows in my Eastside home?

Often, yes. Because much of the Eastside is in a historic district, exterior changes may need city review first, and some projects go before the H-Board. We help you prepare a clear application; the approval decision belongs to the City of Santa Fe.

Should I repair or replace my original wood windows?

It depends on their condition and on the historic-district guidelines for your home. If the frames and sashes are sound, repair can be the better choice. We'll give you an honest assessment and options for both.

Can new windows look like the originals?

In many cases, yes. Wood-clad and other products come in traditional profiles with true or simulated divided lites, and custom sizes and arched tops can match existing openings.
