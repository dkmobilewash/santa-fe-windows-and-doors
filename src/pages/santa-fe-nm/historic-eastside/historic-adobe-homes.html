What reviewers tend to look at

Guidelines differ with each building's status, so treat this as background rather than a rulebook. Common questions include whether original material is being kept, whether the new window matches the old in size and divided-lite pattern, what it's made of, and how visible it is from public streets.

We don't make approval decisions, and we won't promise one. Our job is to give the city a clear, complete picture and to adjust the plan if they ask.

Historic district questions for Eastside owners

How do I find out how my Eastside house is classified?

The city's Historic Preservation Division keeps that information. We can help you look it up early, since a building's classification affects how much flexibility you'll have with windows and doors.

Can windows facing an interior courtyard be handled differently?

Openings that can't be seen from public streets are sometimes treated with more flexibility, but that's up to the city. We'll note visibility for each window in the inventory so the question is easy to ask.

What if the city asks for changes to our plan?

We revise the product selection or details and resubmit. Nothing is ordered until the plan is approved, so a change request costs time rather than money spent on the wrong windows.

Can you work with our architect or preservation consultant?

Yes. We can supply product data, shop drawings and on-site measurements to whoever is leading the application.
