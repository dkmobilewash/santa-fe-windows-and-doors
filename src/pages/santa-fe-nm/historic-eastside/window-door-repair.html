Repairs that suit older Eastside homes

Reglazing and broken lites

Cracked or loose panes in divided-lite sash are reset with fresh glazing, and broken glass is replaced to match. For a historic look, we can source glass with a little of the old waviness.

Painted-shut and swollen sash

Layers of paint and years of seasonal movement lock many old sashes in place. We free them, ease the edges and get them operating without forcing the frame.

Discreet weatherstripping

Low-profile seals fitted into the sash or frame cut drafts and dust while staying out of sight from the lane.

Sagging or dragging wood doors

Heavy old doors drop at the latch side over time. Re-hanging on new hinges, reinforcing loose joints and refitting the strike puts them right.

Why repair often comes first here

For homes in the historic district, keeping original windows in service can be the simplest route through review, and it keeps material that can't be replaced in kind. It's also frequently less disruptive to plaster than pulling a frame out of an adobe wall.

That said, repair has limits. Rot that runs through a sill or bottom rail, or a frame that has pulled away from the wall, can push the decision toward a new unit.

Eastside repair questions

Do repairs on an Eastside house need historic review?

Like-for-like repairs that don't change how a window or door looks are generally treated differently from replacements, but the city's Historic Preservation Division is the authority. We'll flag anything that might need their input.

Can you match the wavy glass in my old windows?

We can usually source restoration glass with a similar look for broken panes. It won't be a perfect match for century-old glass, but it blends in far better than modern float glass.

Our wood windows rattle every time the wind picks up. Is that fixable?

Usually. Loose sash, worn stops and missing weatherstripping are the common causes, and all three can be adjusted or replaced without changing the window's appearance.
