Repair in Santa Fe

Fixing what Santa Fe's sun, wind and monsoon do to windows and doors

Most of the repair calls in this climate trace back to the same few culprits: ultraviolet light that cooks rubber and sealant, grit blown into every track, and summer storms that find any gap. Plenty of those problems can be fixed without replacing the whole unit.

What wears out here, and what we do about it

Sun-baked seals and weatherstripping

At this altitude, UV hardens and cracks rubber gaskets and exterior caulk years sooner than at sea level. We replace the weatherstripping and reseal the frame so it stops whistling on cold nights.

Grit in sliding tracks

Spring wind packs fine sand into slider tracks until the rollers flatten. Cleaning the track, replacing the rollers and adjusting the panel usually brings a heavy door back to a one-finger glide.

Fogged insulated glass

Once the seal on a double-pane unit fails, haze builds up between the panes. If the sash and frame are still solid, a new glass unit sized for the altitude restores the view for less than a new window.

Doors that bind after summer rain

Wood doors take on moisture during the monsoon, and walls can shift a little over the seasons. Planing, re-hanging or adjusting hinges and strikes stops the rubbing without swapping the door.

How a repair visit works

We look at the window or door in question and at the ones next to it, since one failure often points to another on the same wall. You get the price for the repair before we start, and if parts have to be ordered we tell you how long that will take.

Hail damage? Monsoon hail can crack glass and dent screens and cladding. Photos taken right after the storm help with any insurance claim, and we can document the damage when we visit.

Repair questions we get in Santa Fe

Why does my door stick in August but close fine in January?

Wood absorbs moisture during the summer rains and swells, and the frame around it can move a little with the seasons. Adjusting the hinges, strike and threshold, and sometimes planing and resealing the edge, usually solves it for good.

Can you fix a window that was cracked by hail?

In most cases, yes. We replace the broken glass or insulated unit and check the frame and screen for damage. If the window is old and failing in other ways too, we'll mention it so you can decide whether a replacement makes more sense.

Do you work on old steel casement windows?

We'll look at them. Some steel casements can be freed up, resealed and reglazed, while others are too corroded or need parts that no longer exist. We'll tell you which situation you're in before recommending anything.

My slider is impossible to open. Does it need replacing?

Not necessarily. Most hard-to-move sliders have worn rollers or a damaged track, and both can often be replaced. If the frame itself is bent, replacement becomes the better value.
