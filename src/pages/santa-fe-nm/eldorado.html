Homes in Eldorado

Built around the sun

Eldorado, southeast of Santa Fe, is known for homes on large lots with open views, and many of them were designed with passive solar principles: lots of south-facing glass to collect winter sun, thick walls or floors to store the heat, and fewer windows on the north.

Over time, large insulated glass units can fog as their seals fail, original aluminum sliders get drafty and hard to move, and wind on the open land pushes dust through worn weatherstripping.

Planning an Eldorado project

Keep the solar gain

Wind & dust

Open land means steady wind. Careful shimming, insulation and sealing around each frame, plus good weatherstripping, keep dust out.

Insulated glass for your home is ordered with the elevation in mind, and we'll explain how that affects options like gas fills.

Eldorado questions

Will new low-E windows ruin my passive solar design?

Not if the glass is chosen carefully. Low-E coatings vary: some let more of the sun's heat through, others block it. For south-facing glass in a passive solar home, we'll talk through glass that keeps winter solar gain.

Can you replace just the fogged glass in my sunroom?

Often, yes. If the frames are in good shape, replacing the insulated glass units can be a good-value repair. If frames are worn, replacement may be the better investment.

Do I need approval to replace windows in Eldorado?

It may depend on your community's covenants and what you're changing. Check your guidelines; we can provide product details for any approval you need.
