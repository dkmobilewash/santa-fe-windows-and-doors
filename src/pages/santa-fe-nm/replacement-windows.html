Three choices that shape the job

Insert or full-frame

Where the old frame is sound, an insert slips inside it and leaves interior plaster alone. Where frames are rotted, bent or badly out of square, we take everything back to the rough opening.

Frame material

Vinyl keeps cost down on standard openings. Fiberglass shrugs off the swing from a hot afternoon to a freezing night. Wood-clad suits homes where the interior trim is part of the character.

Style per room

Casements seal tightly on windy elevations, picture units frame a mountain view, and double-hungs suit Territorial facades. Mixing styles in one house is normal.

Most homes don't need every window replaced at once. If budget matters, we'll rank your openings by condition so the worst drafts and fogged units get handled first.

Questions from Santa Fe homeowners

Do I need a permit to replace windows in Santa Fe?

It depends on the address and the scope. Changing the size of an opening is treated differently from a same-size swap, and homes in a historic district can need city review before work starts. We look into what applies to your house while preparing the quote, so you aren't left guessing.

Can windows be replaced in the middle of a Santa Fe winter?

Yes. We work one opening at a time, so each room is only open to the cold for a short stretch, and we schedule the day around the forecast where we can.

How far ahead should I plan a window project?

Standard sizes usually arrive faster than custom shapes or wood-clad units, and historic review adds its own time. Your written quote includes the expected order lead time, so you can plan around it.

Will new windows crack my stucco?

Not if they're removed with care. We cut sealant lines rather than prying frames out, and when stucco or plaster does need patching around an opening, we tell you before the work starts rather than after.
