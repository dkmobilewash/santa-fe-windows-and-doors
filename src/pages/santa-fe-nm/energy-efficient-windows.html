Picking Santa Fe glass by the compass, not the catalog

A south window that soaks up December sun and a west window that roasts the kitchen every July afternoon shouldn't have the same glass. In a city with this much sunshine and this many cold nights, which way a window faces matters as much as the window itself.

Comfort problems that point to the windows

  • A chill near the glass on clear winter nights, even with the heat running
  • One room that turns into an oven after about 3 p.m.
  • Faded rugs and floors near south and west windows
  • Frost or condensation on the inside of metal frames
  • A draft you can feel with your hand along the sash

Glass is only half of it

An efficient window installed with gaps around the frame still leaks. We shim and insulate the space between the frame and the wall, then seal inside and out, which is especially important on the windy sides of the house.

We also order insulated glass for Santa Fe's elevation, around 7,000 feet, and explain how that affects gas-fill options for your specific units.

Efficiency questions from Santa Fe homeowners

Is triple-pane glass worth it in Santa Fe?

For many homes, well-chosen double-pane low-E glass in a good frame gets you most of the benefit. Triple-pane can make sense on north walls, in noisy spots, or in very large openings. We're happy to price both so you can compare.

Will low-E glass make my rooms darker?

Modern low-E coatings are nearly clear. Some stronger solar-control coatings do reduce visible light a little, which is why we often use them only on the west side rather than throughout the house.

How much will new windows lower my gas bill?

That depends on what you're replacing, how the house is built and how you heat it, so we won't promise a number. Moving from single-pane metal frames usually makes the biggest difference people notice.
